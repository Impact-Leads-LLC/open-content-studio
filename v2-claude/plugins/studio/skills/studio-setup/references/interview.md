# The interview, round by round

The studio is only as good as what it learns here. Ask one round, listen, write down their words, then the next
round. Skip anything already answered. **Read before you ask:** when a document, a website or a connector answers a
round, take it from there, confirm it in one line, and ask only what it leaves out.

## Round 0: bring what you have (always first)

Ask for everything at once, in one message, and say any of it can be skipped:

- Your website, and the pages that sell your offers.
- A brand guide or style sheet: colours, fonts, logo, tone.
- Offer documents, sales pages, your booking link.
- 5 to 10 posts or emails you wrote yourself.
- Testimonials, case studies, client messages you may use.
- Course, training, workshop or podcast transcripts.
- Your notes or second brain: where it lives (Notion, Google Drive, Obsidian, Dropbox, a custom brain).

Then read it all before Round 1:

- **The website:** fetch the home page and the offer pages with the web tools this app has. Take what they do and for
  whom, the offers (prices only if the site shows them), the booking link, the words they use about their clients,
  the testimonials shown and the social handles. For the look, the site's main colours (its `theme-color`, button and
  heading colours in the CSS) and a linked logo file.
- **Documents:** read each one whole. Word files and PDFs as the skill's Step 2 says.
- **A connector:** look at the tools this session has. If one reaches their notes, search it for offers, frameworks,
  testimonials, best posts and course material (Step 2 saves what's useful into the library). If none does, say so,
  and tell them they can add one in the app's connector settings (they sign in there, never in the chat), or drop the
  files in instead.

Write what you found into a draft of the brand files with where each fact came from. Tell them in a few lines what you
already know and what's still missing. Every later round confirms what you inferred ("Your site says you work with
studios of 5 to 20 people. Still right?") and asks only for the rest. An inference is never written as their words.

## Round 1: who you are and what you sell

- Your name, and the name of the business or brand.
- In one sentence a happy client would say, what do you do for people?
- What do you sell? For each offer: its name, who it's for, what changes for them, the price (and whether the studio
  may mention it), and how someone buys it.
- Do you host a show (a podcast or a video series)? Its name, and where the full episodes live.
- Anything you never talk about, or a claim you never make?

## Round 2: your ideal client, in their words

- Who is the one person you most want to reach? Their role, their situation, the stage their business is at.
- What do they say about the problem when they first talk to you? Exact phrases are gold: paste messages, call notes
  or survey answers if you have them.
- What do they want instead, in their words?
- What do they believe right now that keeps them stuck? What have they already tried?
- What does your market not know yet that your work makes possible? (This feeds the pieces for people who don't know
  they have the problem yet.)

## Round 3: the next step

- When someone is ready, what's the next step? A call (what you call it, exactly, and the booking link), a purchase
  page, an application?
- Lead magnets (a guide, a template, a training)? For each: a one-word keyword, what it is, and how it's delivered
  (people comment the keyword, DM it, or click a link).
- Which network matters most to you? (Captions are written for it first.)

## Round 4: your voice

- Paste 5 to 10 posts or emails you wrote yourself, the ones that sound most like you. Ghost-written ones don't count.
  Say where each came from.
- Lines you use all the time, or a phrase that's yours? Words you never use?
- How do you want to sound to a stranger, in two or three words?
- Hashtags: yes or no? Emoji: never, rarely, or freely?
- From the examples, write 3 to 6 voice rules and read them back ("Short sentences. Calm about money. A dry joke now
  and then."). Ask if that's them.

## Round 5: your ideas and your proof

- Your own frameworks, methods or models: their names and one line each.
- Client results the studio can use. For each: who (and how they may be named: full name, first name, or "a client"),
  the result with its condition ("for a 10-person team", "in the first month"), and where it's written down.
- Anything from Round 0 still to come? Courses, trainings, webinars, podcast episodes (yours and your guest spots),
  past posts, testimonials, case studies, sales pages, FAQs. Ask for all of it, then follow Step 2 of the skill.
- Photos: a headshot for graphics, a logo, and any photos of you working. Only photos of you, or that you own.

## Round 6: your tools and hookups

- What do you record with (Riverside, Zoom, Descript, StreamYard, a phone, a camera)? Does it give you a transcript?
  Where do the recordings end up?
- Where do your files live (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, an external drive)? Should the studio copy videos into its
  folder, or leave them where they are?
- Where do your notes and teaching material live, and which folders or pages hold the good material? (Round 0 found
  out whether this app can reach it.)
- Which scheduler do you post with (Buffer, Metricool, ContentStudio, Later, Hootsuite, Flick, Planoly, or none)? Does
  it take a CSV bulk upload? If so, download its blank template and drop it here.
- How do people get your lead magnets: comment-to-DM (ManyChat or similar), an email tool (which one), or a link?
- Where do people book (Calendly, Cal.com, a form)? The exact link.
- A design tool you keep your brand kit in (Canva, Figma)? The studio makes its own graphics; this only says where your
  logo and colours live.
- Anything the studio must never read or touch?

## Round 7: keys and access

This version needs no keys to run. Two extras are optional:

- **Generated cover pictures** (fal.ai, about 4 cents each): their own fal key, which they put in the Terminal or
  the Keychain themselves. `scripts/picture.mjs` shows how.
- **A connector to their notes:** added in the app's own connector settings, where they sign in.

Say it plainly: no password, key or token ever goes in this chat. If one does, they should replace it where it came
from.

## Round 8: your preferences

- Formats: reels, carousels, text posts. Which ones do you want? (A format turned off isn't planned.)
- Which networks for each format? Defaults: reels to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn and Facebook;
  carousels to Instagram, LinkedIn and Facebook; text posts to LinkedIn, Facebook and Threads.
- Run size: full (5 to 8 reels, 10 carousels and 10 posts from an episode) or light (about half)? See the plan before
  anything is built (recommended), or go straight through?
- Your look: confirm the colours from Round 0, or give hex codes. Fonts (the files, if you have them), logo. No
  colours yet: the studio uses deep blue `#1E4E6E` with a yellow `#FFC530` accent until you choose. Step 4 shows you
  the result.
- Reels: the longest you want (default 75 seconds), captions in capitals or not, the whole frame or a crop on you.
- Generated cover pictures when a carousel has no video still: yes or no?
- Who reviews before you: only you, or someone on your team first?
- Anything else you love or hate seeing in content.

Write each answer where it takes effect: `settings.json` for formats, networks, run size and the plan step;
`palette.json` for the look; the matching playbook's "Your changes" for craft rules ("60 seconds at most" goes in
`reels.md`).

## Round 9: spend and time

- Which Claude plan are you on (Pro, Max, Team)? A full episode run is a big job. On Pro, video-heavy days can hit the
  usage limit; light runs fit Pro better, and Max suits a full episode a week.
- Generated pictures: a monthly ceiling (about $5 a month covers 125 pictures). fal works on credit you buy, so buy
  only that much.
- Your scheduler's plan, and any limit it has on posts, networks or bulk uploads.
- Time: how many minutes a day will you spend reviewing? Allow about two minutes a piece on the board. Fifteen minutes
  a day suits three pieces a day.

Write it in `settings.json` under `budget`, then set `mix` and `rhythm.json` to what the budget and the time support,
and say what you chose and why.

## Round 10: rhythm and time zone

- Which city's time are you on? (Write the time zone as a name like America/Chicago.)
- How many pieces a day, and at what times? Default: three a day at 9:00, 12:00 and 17:00, one reel, one carousel and
  one text post. Which days? Any slot to keep free (for example Sunday 9:00 for the full episode)?

## Round 11: clearing

Once a piece is marked posted, its renders (videos, slides, graphics) are deleted 7 days later and its words, plans
and reviews stay. An episode's raw footage (only copies inside the studio folder) goes once every piece from it is
posted or dropped. Nothing unposted is ever touched. Keep that, change the 7 days, or move the files to an archive
folder (an external drive, or a synced Google Drive or Dropbox folder) instead of deleting them?
