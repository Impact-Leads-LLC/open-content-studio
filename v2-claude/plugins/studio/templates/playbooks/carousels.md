# Carousels: one idea, slide by slide

Carousels are the format people save most: steps, a method, a story with its lesson. The default is the story
carousel, and almost every idea fits it.

## The story carousel

1. **The cover** (slide 1): the hook as one sentence over three lines, its key words huge in your accent colour, and a
   small sticker saying what the reader gets ("One line in your proposal fixes it"). It has to work as a thumbnail.
2. **Six to eight reading slides**: one point per slide, in full sentences, each slide leading to the next so the
   reader keeps swiping. Each slide has one bold line: the one that lands.
3. **A save reminder** on slide 3 or 4, where people decide whether it's worth keeping.
4. **The outro**: a headline tied to the topic, your next step as a button, and what following you gets them.

## What makes slides work

- One point per slide. A slide that needs two bold lines is two slides.
- The speaker's own words and figures, and your teaching from the library for the steps.
- No recap slide and no call-to-action slide: the outro does both jobs.
- Steps numbered across slides, three at most on one slide.
- A guest's idea credited to the guest on every slide it appears on.

## When to use another format

Only when the idea is a thing to show: an email or message someone described, a before and after, a page, one striking
number. Claude then designs those slides in your look, rebuilding what was described in the speaker's own words.
Never an invented email, message, figure or name.
