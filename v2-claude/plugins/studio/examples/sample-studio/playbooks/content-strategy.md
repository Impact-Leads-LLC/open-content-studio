# Content strategy: what to make, for whom, and why

The logic behind every piece. The studio reads this before it plans anything. Change it to fit how you sell.

## Every piece has one job

One reader, one idea, one next step. Each piece moves one reader one step closer to buying from you. A piece that
teaches two things, or asks for two things, does neither.

## The five awareness levels

Your audience isn't one crowd. People sit at different distances from buying, and each distance needs a different
piece. The studio plans every piece for one of these five levels (Eugene Schwartz's levels of awareness).

| Level | What the reader is thinking | What the piece does | Works best as | It asks for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | "Things are fine." | Opens the idea with a story, a surprising result, or the idea itself | Reels, story posts, short hook posts | Save, follow, or the full episode |
| Problem-aware | "Something's off and I don't know why." | Names the problem in their words and shows what it costs | Teaching posts, carousels on one mistake | Save, or a lead magnet |
| Solution-aware | "I know there's a fix. Which one, and how?" | Shows how it works: the steps, the mistakes, the numbers | How-to carousels and posts | A lead magnet |
| Product-aware | "I know you. Is it for me?" | Proof: a client result, how the work goes, an objection answered | Client-result and objection posts | Your call |
| Most aware | "I'm close." | The reason to act now, and what happens next | Invitation posts | Your call |

Most of your audience sits at the top two levels, so most of what reaches new people is built for them. The bottom
two levels need real proof and a real offer from your material. The studio never invents either; when your material
has none, it tells you that level came up short.

The ids the studio's files use: `unaware`, `problem`, `solution`, `product`, `most`.

## The save test

The best piece is one people save to use later: steps they can follow, a script they can copy, a checklist, numbers
they can use. When two ideas are close, make the one people would save.

## One insight, many angles

A good insight is worth more than one piece. Tell it several ways: an unaware story, a problem-aware wake-up, a
solution-aware how-to, a product-aware proof, a most-aware reason to book. Each gets its own hook and its own format,
so the feed never repeats itself while the idea sinks in.

## How much from one source

A full episode (20 minutes or more of video) gives 5 to 8 reels, 10 carousels and 10 posts: two carousels and two
posts for each level. A short clip, a transcript, or a topic from your library gives less, and nothing without video
becomes a reel. `mix` in `brand/settings.json` is `full` or `light` (half). Reels are held to a score, because writing
can't rescue a weak clip. Carousels and posts are built out from your material, so they're kept unless they're untrue
or off your lane. You see the plan before anything is built, and you can drop or add pieces there.

## Posting several times a day

The default rhythm is three a day (a reel, a carousel and a text post) at 9:00, 12:00 and 17:00 in your time zone, set
in `brand/rhythm.json`. The calendar spreads the levels and the insights so two pieces on one idea never go out back
to back, and every level shows up each week. Fewer is fine: one good piece a day beats three tired ones.

## Pillars

Three to five topics you want to be known for, listed in `brief.md`. Each week touches each pillar. A piece outside
every pillar gets cut, however good it is.

## The next step, by level

One ask per piece, at the end, after a short line asking people to save it.

- Unaware and problem-aware: save it, follow, the full episode, or a lead magnet.
- Solution-aware: a lead magnet.
- Product-aware and most aware: your call, by its exact name.

A lead magnet has a one-word keyword in capitals (in `settings.json`). People comment it, DM it, or click the link,
the way you deliver it. No lead magnet or published episode yet? Then every level points to your call, asked lightly
("Want a second look at your next hire?"), and the save line does the light work. A lead magnet is the best next
thing to make: it gives the top three levels a step that fits them.

## Never make

- A recap of the episode with nothing to keep.
- A piece for everyone.
- An announcement that gives the reader nothing.
- A claim, number, quote or client story that isn't in your material.
- A trend that sits outside your pillars.
