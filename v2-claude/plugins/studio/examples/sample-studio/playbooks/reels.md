# Reels: which moments, how they're cut, and their covers

## What makes a reel moment

The clip has to stop a scroll, teach, and land on its own, with no context. The best ones open on:

- a strong opinion said plainly,
- a number with an outcome,
- a named mistake,
- a story with a turn,
- the reader's exact problem, in their words.

A guest talking alone, or you only asking a question, rarely works as a reel. Reels are the one format held to a
score, because writing can't rescue a weak clip.

## Cutting

- Start on the hook sentence: never mid-sentence, never on "so, yeah".
- End where the point lands and the sentence finishes.
- 20 to 75 seconds. Shorter usually wins.
- Long pauses are tightened; a pause that's part of the point stays.
- Captions three words at a time, the word being said in the accent colour, clear of the face.
- The cover line in a box for the first three seconds, so the reel works with the sound off.
- From landscape video: the whole frame over a blurred fill keeps everyone in shot. A crop on one speaker only when
  they carry the moment and stay in one spot.

## Covers

The cover line (`hooks.md`) set large over a still where your face is clear, with the words away from your face.
Rotate the three treatments across an episode (`design.md`).

## What the studio leaves to your editor

B-roll, music, animated graphics and cut-outs. Take `reel.mp4` into CapCut, Descript or Premiere for those.
