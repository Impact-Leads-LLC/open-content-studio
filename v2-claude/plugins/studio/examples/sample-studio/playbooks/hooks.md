# Hooks: first lines and cover lines

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second. The `hooks` skill first pulls the raw material out of the
source (its claims, figures, beliefs, costs, stories and quotable lines), then writes from it the way this page says.
The best hook is usually already in the source as a line someone said.

## Five ways in

Every set uses five different ways in, so you get real choices instead of five rewordings of one line.

1. **Flat claim.** The idea said plainly, with no hedge. Often the strongest. "Send the first invoice the day the work
   starts."
2. **Wrong belief.** What the reader believes, then the evidence against it. "Most studio owners think more clients
   fix cash. Their best month last year still ran out on the 20th." Never "it's not X, it's Y".
3. **Specific.** A figure, a place, a date or a named thing first. "60 days: that's how long your client holds your
   payroll."
4. **Reader or cost.** Call out the exact reader ("If you've ever checked the bank balance before opening your email")
   or lead with what it cost ("Waiting to invoice cost me a year of weekends").
5. **Story or quote.** Start inside the moment, or open with the line someone said, word for word, and one line of
   context.

## Rules for every hook

- One idea. Two ideas halve both.
- The topic is clear in the first few words; only the answer is held back.
- A concrete noun early: a number, an object, a role, a place.
- Promise only what the piece pays off.
- Name the reader precisely ("studio owners", "new coaches"), never "people".
- A question only when the reader is already asking that exact question and can't answer it in a word.

## Never open with

- "Here's how", "Here's what", "The result?", "The key is", "Stop doing".
- "Changed everything", "once and for all", "overnight", "no one talks about", "the truth no one tells you".
- Throat-clearing: "Quick thought", "Let's talk about", "I've been thinking".
- A cover that needs the video to make sense: "Wait for it", "Watch till the end".

## Cover lines

The words on reel and carousel covers. A stranger scrolling your grid, with no sound and no caption, should know what
they'll learn. 3 to 8 words, one phrase or one short sentence, never two fragments in a row. Sentence case, no full
stop, no hashtags, no emoji. One accent: the most loaded word or number.
