# Full Episode Edits

How a full podcast episode is produced for YouTube and the podcast feed. The episode editor reads this before it plans
an edit, and it wins over the editor's built-in defaults. This is a starting house style: change anything that
doesn't fit your show, and keep it short and plain.

## What every episode gets

Every produced episode gets all of this without anyone asking:

- The camera follows whoever is talking. Both people side by side when they talk over each other, at the open and the
  close, and now and then during a long answer.
- A slow push in (or out) on any single shot of 8 seconds or more, and short punch-ins on the lines that land.
- A cold open, a title card, a name card for each guest, chapter cards, an end card.
- A graphic every two or three minutes.
- Word-by-word captions with key words in a box.
- Levelled sound.

## The type of episode

Say what kind of episode it is, on the guest's card and the title card:

- Masterclass: the host teaching on their own.
- Free coaching session: the host coaching the guest live on the guest's own problem.
- Expert guest: the guest teaches what they know.

Add the topic in 2 to 6 plain words, for example "How to generate leads".

## The title

- A number and an outcome, in the listener's words.
- Under 60 characters.
- No vague insider titles. If a stranger can't tell what they get, rewrite it.
- It may differ from the working title.

## The opening (30 to 40 seconds)

1. One to three sound bites from the middle of the episode that make people want to stay: a sharp claim, a number, a
   turn in a story. Each 4 to 10 seconds, 10 to 18 seconds together, each starting and ending on a full sentence.
2. The host introducing the guest: the tightest 5 to 22 seconds (name and what they do). Cut the small talk.
3. The ad read, if the host reads one in the intro, with the offer on screen.
4. The title card, then the episode starts after the introduction and the hellos, so nothing from the opening is
   heard twice.

## Chapters

- 4 to 8 chapters, each a topic change, at least 60 seconds apart.
- Titles of 2 to 6 plain words.
- They become chapter cards in the video and the chapters in the YouTube description.

## Name cards

Everyone on screen who isn't the host gets introduced the first time they're on screen alone and talking:

- Who they are, under 45 characters (for example "Founder, Brightline Studio").
- Why the viewer should trust them, from the episode, under 50 characters (for example "12 years in brand design").
- Whether they're a guest or someone the host is working with.

## Graphics

- One every two or three minutes: 10 to 15 in a 30-minute episode, fewer in a short one.
- Each one lands on the line it shows and stays 6 to 12 seconds, at least 25 seconds from the next.
- Never in the first 10 seconds or the last 15.
- Kinds: a list or checklist (2 to 5 short rows, each appearing as it's said), a big number (exactly as said), a
  comparison (only real numbers from the episode), a quote (word for word, under 20 words, with who said it), a message
  (an email or text as described in the episode), a website.
- Keep the words short: rows under 32 characters, titles under 28.
- When a tool, a website or a product comes up by name, show its real homepage, unless a screen share already shows it.
  Never an app's sign-in screen. Up to 6 a show.

## Never flat

- Pops: 8 to 16 stickers of 2 to 5 words, said word for word, that hover beside the speaker for about 3 seconds on a
  line worth remembering.
- Punch-ins: 8 to 20, each 1.5 to 4 seconds, on a number, a sharp claim or a punchline. Gentle, never a sharp snap.
- Never more than 25 seconds without a graphic, a chapter card, a pop, a punch-in or a screen share.

## The look

- Mostly The Board: the video slides back into a framed window and the content takes the screen on the brand's main
  colour, with big white type and ink chips.
- A splash of Kinetic Layers: stickers and numbers that hover over the footage beside the speaker. Names and pops are
  always kinetic; about one graphic in four is too.
- Never the same layout twice in a row.
- The accent colour is an accent only (numbers, chapter numbers, the booking button), never a background.
- The speaker's face is never covered.

## Captions

- Word by word as each word is said, low in the frame.
- 15 to 40 key words in a box: the word a line turns on (a number, a name, the punchline). At most one per sentence.
- Numbers turn to the accent colour on their own.
- Fix names, brands and numbers the speech-to-text got wrong.
- Off while the title card and the end card are up.

## The end card

- One line that says what the call to action gets the viewer, in their words, so it's clear what they're signing up for.
- The call to action and its page, from the Brand Brief.
- The ad read's offer on screen uses the same line.

## Sound

- Both voices levelled to each other, gently compressed and limited, then set to -14 LUFS for YouTube.
- A soft whoosh at each join in the opening and under each chapter card.

## Truth

- Every word on screen comes from the episode or the Brand Brief.
- Never invent a figure, a client, a quote or a claim.
- Ideas from a guest belong to the guest: credit them by name.
- Follow the Voice Guardrails: no em dashes, no hype.
