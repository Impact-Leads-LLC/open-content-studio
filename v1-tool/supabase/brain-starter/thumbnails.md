# Thumbnails

How this studio's YouTube thumbnails look and what they say. The thumbnail engine reads this document every time it
writes thumbnail words, and it wins over the engine's own defaults. Edit it to match your channel: change a rule
here, not in the code.

## What a thumbnail is for

The title says what the video covers. The thumbnail adds the charge: the stake, the warning, the result, the regret or
the contradiction. A viewer should get it in under a second at phone size, with no sound and no context.

## The words

- The words come from the episode's biggest takeaway: the best advice in it, the thing the host spent the most time
  teaching, the thing a viewer would come away using. Never a side tactic or a fun aside, however good its number is.
  A memorable number about a side trick belongs to its own short video.
- The thumbnail never repeats a content word from the title.
- Five words at most: a support line (two words at most), a hero (one word), and a sticker or tag (two words at most)
  for the payoff.
- All caps. The only punctuation is an apostrophe, a question mark (only for a question the viewer is already asking),
  a percent sign and a hyphen. No commas, no dashes, no dollar sign (write the figure another way).
- No contrast lines ("not X, it's Y").
- Every figure on a thumbnail is one said in the episode. A round figure from the speaker is flagged so the exact one
  can be swapped in.
- An idea from a guest belongs to the guest. Never put a guest's claim in the host's mouth.
- The words promise only what the episode pays off.

## The three looks

The studio rotates three looks so the channel page doesn't look like one thumbnail repeated. The look published least
lately goes first.

- **The call.** The guest's real webcam frame from the episode in a white window, the host in front of it on the
  right, the problem on a dark tag over the window and the fix as the big word below it, with one loop around it in
  the accent colour. Use it when the guest's face or gesture at that moment shows the problem.
- **The number wall.** A giant number behind the host's head, one or two words in front of it, and the payoff on an
  accent sticker. Use it when the takeaway has a number worth seeing big (one to five characters).
- **The board.** A tilted card in the brand's main colour with the words on it and the payoff on an accent tag, the
  host in a white print beside it. It plays with almost any words, and with a photo that has no cutout.

## Colour

- One hot colour per thumbnail: the brand's accent, on one element (the loop, the sticker or the tag). The accent is
  never a background.
- The board and the backdrop use the brand's main colour, deepened when white type wouldn't read on it.
- Text on the accent is dark or white, whichever reads better.

## The host's photos

- Always the owner's own photos from the photo library, never a generated or edited face. A photo is cut out and
  colour-graded as a whole; nothing inside the face is ever touched, mirrored or rotated.
- Each photo is tagged with the expressions it plays: skeptic, verdict, warning, number, secret, smirk, how. The words
  are written only to expressions a photo exists for today. To open up more hooks, add photos with new expressions.
- A good thumbnail photo: one person, the face sharp and large (head and shoulders, room above the hair), both eyes
  visible, looking at the lens or into the frame, a clear expression, a plain background.
- Mark which side of the frame the person should sit on, so they face into the frame. The call needs the person on
  the right.
- A photo with a ready cutout or a mask works in every look. Without one, the engine tries to cut it out; if it can't,
  that photo plays only the board.

## Layout rules the engine checks

- The bottom-right corner stays clear (YouTube puts the video length there).
- Nothing within about 48 px of the host's face at full size, and nothing covering the guest's face.
- Every word still reads at 168 px wide, the size of a thumbnail in a crowded feed.
- Type never shrinks to fit. Words that are too wide go back for fewer letters.

## Before you pick one

- Cover the words. Does the expression, or the guest's gesture, still carry the hook?
- Look at the feed sheet, dark and light. The thumbnail should not be the darkest tile.
- Truth test: the words promise only what the video pays off, and they say the biggest takeaway.
