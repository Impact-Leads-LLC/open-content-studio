# Reel Edits

How this studio's short vertical reels are cut and dressed. Two editors read this document before they plan a reel:
the reel editor (a clip cut from an episode's camera feeds) and the animated reel editor (a reel made from words only,
with no footage). It wins over their built-in defaults. This is a starting house style: change anything that doesn't
fit your channel, and keep it short and plain.

## What a reel is

- One idea, 20 to 60 seconds. Up to about 80 seconds when the host's own point is the ending.
- 9:16, captions on, made to work with the sound off.
- It opens on a line that stops the scroll and ends where the point lands.

## The cut

- Keep the best take of each line. Drop false starts, restarts, filler and pauses longer than about half a second.
  Cut in the pause between words, never inside one.
- Keep the speakers' words and their order. Never stitch a sentence together that nobody said.
- Start on a line that hooks: a claim, a number, a turn in a story. Not a greeting, not "so".
- The host test: a reel with nothing of the host in it fails. When the host makes their own point in the clip, keep
  their best take of it, usually as the ending, and cut the guest's setup harder instead.

## The opening bar

- Optional: a short plain sentence over the first seconds, about 40 characters at most.
- A number and an outcome in the words a founder would use ("One podcast a month made $10,000").
- A number keeps what the speaker attached to it ("a month", "per lead"). An example stays an example.
- Never a fragment label or the guest's jargon.

## The camera

- One face at a time: the camera cuts to whoever is talking. A jump cut shows both people for a moment, then moves
  back in.
- No punch-in on every cut. At most one or two hard punches on the single word that lands hardest, and one or two
  slow pushes on the lines that matter most.
- A zoom-blur through a join only when the reel moves to a new idea (one or two a reel at most).

## Graphics

- Three to five per reel, each on one of the strongest lines, never the same kind twice. Leave a second or more of
  plain talking head between them.
- Pick the kind from what is said: a list when the speaker names things, a number box when a number matters, a
  message mockup when they read out an email or DM, a checklist when they give steps, a bar comparison only when the
  source gives real numbers for both sides.
- Use the effect library in reels of 40 seconds or more (at least two effects instead of plain boxes): the app on
  screen, names circling the speaker, a wall of moments, quotes pinned up behind them, a chart that grows, a magazine
  cover, one glass word in the room, a paper collage, a hand-drawn doodle, a social post frame, the room falling away
  on a realization. At most three effects that put something behind the speaker.
- An effect has to show what is said. It never decorates.
- Every word and number on screen comes from the transcript, in the speaker's words. The boxes never repeat the
  opening bar.
- A box title is 1 to 3 words. A list has at most four short rows.

## Captions

- Big, word by word, with whoever is talking.
- Two to five key words light up (the idea words), never filler.
- The captions are the speakers' words. The only change allowed is fixing a word the speech-to-text misheard, numbers
  and names above all.

## The look

- Natural footage with a light grade. No heavy dark-room grade.
- Graphics are white cards with ink edges and hard shadows, on a board in the brand's primary colour with a faint dot
  grid. The accent colour is for small moments only: the hanging tag, a key word, the end card's button.
- The reel opens on its cover for about half a second, then slides into the content.

## Animated reels (no footage)

- The cover for half a second, then 6 to 12 beats of animated type at a comfortable reading pace, then the end card.
- One idea per beat, in the source's own words. Statement, number, list, before and after, quote, card and kinetic
  type beats, mixed so no two in a row look the same.
- With a voiceover, the beats follow the voice. Without one, the reel is set at reading pace.

## The end card

- Guest clips: the guest's name, "Guest on <your show> with <your name>", the button "Full episode" and "Link in bio".
- Clips with no guest and animated reels: your name, a short line on what the call to action gets the viewer, the
  button (for example "Book a free call") and where it goes (for example "Link in bio" or your booking page).
- Change the wording here to set your own end card.

## Words on screen

- Plain words, full sentences where a sentence fits. No em dashes.
- Follow the Voice Guardrails. Never invent a figure, a client, a quote or a claim.
- Ideas from a guest are credited to the guest by name and never shown as the host's advice.
- A document marked confidential is never quoted. A recording of the host as a guest on someone else's show is for
  their stories and voice, never the teaching.
