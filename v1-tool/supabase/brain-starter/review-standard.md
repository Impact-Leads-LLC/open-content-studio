# Review Standard

The standard every piece meets before it reaches the owner. The studio's reviewer (Coach <owner>) judges against it, and
so does anyone on the team checking work. Keep the seven checks, their ids and their order (the app shows them as
cards); change what each one asks for. It sits on top of the brand brief and the voice file. Where they disagree, the
voice file wins.

## The bar

A piece can pass every written rule and still not be worth posting. Rules check the words. They can't tell whether the
reader would stop for it, or whether the owner is in it at all. So the checks run in priority order, and the written
rules come last.

## The seven checks, in order

1. **Stop test** (`stop`). Does the first line give the reader a number and an outcome in their own words, without
   needing to know who the guest is? A guest's name means nothing to the audience, so it can't be the hook.
2. **Owner test** (`owner`). Is there at least one sentence only the owner would say: their stake, their story, or a
   stand tied to the reader's problem? A recap of the guest's point fails. When this fails, the reviewer never invents
   the owner's view. It asks the owner one question instead, for example: "What do you believe about this that your
   guest doesn't?"
3. **Truth test** (`truth`). Does every number, quote and claim match the source, with its original condition, owner
   and scope? Keep whose client it was, which list it was, and the exact wording. Nothing appears that isn't in the
   source. An inference is never stated as a fact.
4. **Right-person test** (`person`). Does every name card, credit and label sit on the person it names, and is the
   person talking visible and uncovered? A guest's ideas credit the guest. The owner's view sits on its own "my take"
   slide or paragraph, in their words. A guest's advice never appears under the owner's face.
5. **New-each-time test** (`fresh`). One point, one proof. Every slide, beat or paragraph adds something the last one
   didn't. No repeated stat, and no stacking three witnesses for one point.
6. **Door test** (`door`). Does it end on one next step that fits this reader, in plain words, readable at phone size?
   The doors are in the brand brief. One door, never three.
7. **Lint** (`lint`). The voice file's banned phrases, no em dashes, no invented casual filler, no manufactured sales
   lines, a fifth-grade reading level.

## Format standards

- **Posts:** the first line works alone above the "see more" fold. Full sentences, one idea told well.
- **Carousels:** the visual type comes from the content (an email or DM mockup, a guest feature, a chart, side by side,
  steps, a quote; a list only when it really is a list). Every slide reads at phone size. Nothing is clipped at an edge.
- **Reels:** a natural room look, about three graphics per reel, captions inside the safe zones, graphics never over the
  speaker's face, name cards on the right person, and an end card with one door.
- **Titles and hooks:** a number, a concrete outcome and the buyer's words.

## Verdicts

- **Ready:** checks 1 to 6 pass and any lint is fixed.
- **Fix first:** one or more of checks 3 to 7 fail and the fix is clear. The reviewer gives the exact line before and
  after.
- **Rethink:** check 1 or 2 fails, or the piece has no reason to exist for this audience.
