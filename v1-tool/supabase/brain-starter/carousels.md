# Carousels

How this studio's Instagram and LinkedIn carousels look and read. The carousel builder reads this document before it
plans a deck, and it wins over the builder's built-in skill. This is a starting house style: change anything that
doesn't fit your brand, and keep it short and plain.

## What a carousel is for

- One idea people want to keep: steps, a script, a plan, the exact words to say. Make it savable first.
- 1080x1350 slides, 10 at most including the outro (schedulers take at most 10 images a post).

## Pick the format

- **Story (the default):** for a story, steps, a lesson or a list. One graphic cover, 6 to 8 plain reading slides,
  the outro. Most ideas go here.
- **Artifact:** only when the idea is a thing to show: an email or DM someone sends, a page, a side-by-side, a stat.
  Show the thing itself, mark what makes it work, then the result.
- Never force an idea into a heavy template. A graphic on the front card and plain text after it beats images on
  every slide.

## The cover

- The hook as one sentence across three lines: a few small words, 1 to 3 key words set huge in caps (the one accent
  moment), a few small words to finish.
- A number, an outcome and the reader's words. No vague insider titles.
- A still from the episode in a framed window: the speaker mid-gesture on the moment the hook comes from. With no
  episode still, a generated picture of one object or place from the topic, never a person.
- A small sticker with what the reader gets.
- The episode type or show in a chip at the top ("Masterclass", "Expert guest", "Free coaching session" or your show's
  name).

## The reading slides

- Light background, dark text. Never light text on a dark background for slides people read.
- Two or three short blocks per slide, 120 to 220 characters in all, and exactly one bold line that lands.
- Full sentences at a fifth-grade reading level. Each slide leads to the next. No recap slide.
- Steps are numbered and run on across slides, three at most on a slide.
- A save pill on the 3rd or 4th slide, once: "Save this for ..." with something specific.

## The outro (every deck)

- Your photo and name, "Host of <your show>".
- A headline tied to the topic (a question is fine).
- One button with your call to action (for example "Book a free strategy call"), and "Link in bio".
- "Save this post for later." and "Follow <your handle> for more <topic> like this."

## Guests

- Credit the guest on the cover and on every slide that carries their idea.
- Their words and figures stay exactly as the transcript has them. Never write their advice as the host's.
- An artifact deck gets a separate take slide for the host, drafted from what the host said, flagged for the host to
  rewrite.

## Photos

- Episode stills at their own size or smaller, never blown up.
- The host's photos come from the brain's photo library, never generated.
- Never stock photos, generated people, or a guest's headshot from their site without asking.

## Words

- Follow the Voice, the Voice Guardrails and the Avoid AI Writing audit on every word.
- No em dashes or en dashes. No "not X, it's Y" lines. No stacks of fragments. No hype.
- Headlines are statements, never topics.
- Never invent an email, a figure, a name or a result. A document marked confidential is never quoted.

## The look

- Libre Franklin for headings and the big number, Poppins for everything else.
- The cover and outro on the brand's primary colour; the accent only on the cover's key words and the button.
- Small dots for bullets. No clip-art icons. A plain check and cross only on a slide that sorts good from bad.
