# LinkedIn Content Skill

Write LinkedIn posts inside a fixed system: post-type rotation, cadence, hook discipline and one engineered call to action. The owner's voice and subject matter go into that system.

**The governing rule: structure from this skill, everything else from the brand documents.** Voice, audience, subject matter, offers, proof, named ideas and keywords all come from the brand documents. Never bring in hype, income promises or urgency theater.

---

## Step 0: Read the brand documents (every run)

The brand documents live in the brain. Load them through the brain connector before writing anything (get_brand_brief,
then brain_get_document for any document it names). They are the authority on everything this skill doesn't own:

- **The brand brief ("Brand Brief"):** the audience, the subject matter, the offers, the next step each piece points to (including any comment keywords and what each one gets the reader), the proof (client results that may be cited) and the owner's named ideas.
- **The voice files ("Voice", "Voice Guardrails"):** tone, reading level, words to use, banned phrases and banned framings.
- **The review standard ("Review Standard"):** the seven checks every post is judged on (stop, owner, truth, right person, new each time, door, lint). Write to pass them.
- **The avoid-AI-writing audit ("Avoid AI Writing"):** the humanizer pass in step 6 of the workflow.

This skill supplies the structure: the post types, the ratio, hook discipline, the call-to-action mechanism and LinkedIn formatting. Where the skill and the brand documents disagree, the brand documents win.

If the brief names the owner's frameworks or methods, use those exact names, and only where the material actually touches them. Never coin a new named framework.

## How this skill runs in the studio

The studio app runs this skill unattended. Nobody is there to answer questions, so the intake is already settled:

- **Scope:** one post per run.
- **Raw material:** the episode transcript excerpt in the message. Every fact, number, story and quote in the post comes from that excerpt or from the brand documents.
- **Angle:** the chosen concept sets what the post is about.
- **Type:** if the app names a type, use it. If not, pick the type the material supports (below).

**Whose idea is it.** Keep track of who said what in the excerpt.

- An idea a guest says belongs to the guest. Credit them by name in the post and never present their advice as the owner's.
- The owner's own take comes only from what the owner says in the excerpt or in the brand documents. Use their words, or a close paraphrase.
- Never invent the owner's opinions, stories, experiences, clients or numbers. Every number keeps its original condition and its owner (who said it, about what, under what circumstances).
- If the post needs something the source doesn't have, leave it out or choose a type that works without it. If a line needs the owner's check, say so in the notes the app collects outside the post.

**When the idea is a guest's,** a teaching post usually fits best: name the guest early, give their point in their words, and add the owner's take only if the owner gave one in the excerpt.

**Picking the type when the app doesn't name one:**

- Short hook and teaching posts work from almost any excerpt.
- Personal and story posts need a real story the owner tells in the excerpt.
- A case study needs a real client result from the excerpt or the brief's proof, with its real numbers.
- Invites and launch posts need real logistics (a date, a cap, a close date) from the brief or the app. Without them, pick another type.
- Offer posts need a keyword offer in the brief that fits the topic.
- When several types fit, lean toward what the ratio needs most. Hooks and teaching posts make up most of a healthy feed.

## The ratio and cadence (how the feed is built over time)

Each run writes one post, but the feed works as a whole. Out of every ~10 posts:

- **4 short hook posts** (35 words or fewer): the attention layer
- **3 authority posts:** teaching with no ask, including **1 client case study every 2 weeks**
- **1 to 2 invitation posts:** a workshop, live training or replay
- **1 to 2 conversion posts:** story + offer, or teaching + offer
- **About 1 personal post** in every 12 or so

Launch and scarcity posts exist only during a real launch with a real cap or close date.

**Cadence:** on a heavy day, pair one substantive post with one short hook that points to it. Run a single theme for 1 to 3 weeks and hit it from several angles (teach it, prove it with a case study, tease it with a hook, then invite people to the next step) instead of saying it once. One episode often feeds several of those angles; the concept tells you which one this run is.

## The eight post types

Skeletons, rules, hook formulas and structure examples are in `references/post-types.md`. **Read that file before writing any post.** Summary:

1. **Short hook post:** one line, or two or three short lines. A contrarian claim, a number or a curiosity gap. Often the caption on a carousel or video. No ask, or a single pointer ("swipe", "video below").
2. **Belief / teaching (no offer):** mid-length. Teach one principle. End on a clean takeaway. No ask.
3. **Workshop / live invite:** promote a live session or its replay. One date, one ask.
4. **Personal / lifestyle:** a real moment from the owner's life, tied loosely to a business truth. No ask. These earn the most engagement, and that attention funds the offer posts.
5. **Personal story + offer:** the flagship. First-person story, then the turn, the lesson stated plainly, a bridge to the reader and one ask.
6. **Teaching + offer:** teach something genuinely useful, then offer the deeper free resource through comment-a-keyword.
7. **Client case study:** "How [client] went from [before] to [after]." Problem, what changed, results, what the owner respects about them, one ask. The highest-engagement business post.
8. **Launch / scarcity:** only during a real, capped launch. One honest deadline, no manufactured urgency.

## The call to action: one engineered ask

Conversion posts use one mechanism:

> **"Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [the free thing]."**

- One ask only. Never stack asks.
- Lead with value. The free thing (a training, a breakdown, a checklist) is useful on its own and comes without a pitch.
- The keyword and what it gets the reader come from the brand brief. Pick the one that matches the post's topic and write the keyword in capitals exactly as given.
- Never invent a keyword, a free resource, a link, a price or a program name. If the brief has no keyword offer that fits, end on the plain next step the brief or the app names instead.
- The ladder behind the keyword (free resource, then a conversation, then the paid offer) lives in the brief. Never hard-code names or prices.
- Vary the ask across the feed so it isn't all "comment KEYWORD." Some posts invite a direct message. Authority and personal posts carry no sales ask; if the app requires every post to end on a next step, give those posts the lightest one it allows (for example, where to hear the full conversation), stated plainly.

Never: income promises ("DM me to hit $20K months"), countdown timers, invented scarcity ("only 3 spots left") or guaranteed results.

## Voice

The voice file sets the voice. Follow it over anything here. These craft defaults apply only where it is silent:

- Plain, short words. One idea per sentence. Active voice.
- Talk to the reader as a peer, never down to them.
- Specific beats inspirational, every time. Real numbers and real scenarios from the source. If a claim can't be made specific, cut it.
- Lead with understanding. The reader should feel seen before any method or offer appears.
- No hype, no income promises, no fear-based selling, no urgency theater. Scarcity only when it's real and named in the brief.
- Skip stock coaching phrases (game-changer, unlock your potential, proven system, mindset shift, journey) and every AI tell the audit lists.

## LinkedIn formatting

- **The first line sits above the fold.** LinkedIn shows only the first two or three lines (fewer on a phone) before "...see more". The hook has to work there on its own, so keep line one short: under about 15 words.
- **One idea per line.** Short paragraphs of one or two sentences, with a blank line between them. White space does a lot of the work.
- **Plain text only.** LinkedIn doesn't render markdown: no asterisks, no # headers, no unicode bold. Case-study labels sit on their own line, ending with a colon.
- **Lists:** one item per line, starting with a dash. Two to four items.
- **Emoji:** at most one, usually none.
- **Hashtags:** no padding. Follow the app and the brand documents, which may ban them.
- **Length by type:** short hooks 35 words or fewer; teaching 120 to 280 words; story + offer 250 to 450 words. LinkedIn's hard limit is 3,000 characters.

## Workflow

1. **Intake:** read the excerpt and the concept. Find the one idea and the one feeling the reader should have. Note who said what: the guest's ideas, the owner's own take, and every number with its condition.
2. **Pick the type:** the app's choice, or the best fit for the material.
3. **Draft the hook:** write two or three first-line options, then keep the strongest. The first line is the whole game: a door into the post, never a summary of it. Hook formulas are in `references/post-types.md`.
4. **Build the body** in the type's skeleton. One idea per line. Specific numbers from the source. Credit the guest wherever the idea is theirs.
5. **Set the call to action:** one ask, matched to the post type and the brief's next steps. Authority and personal posts get no sales ask.
6. **Humanizer pass (mandatory):** run the post through the avoid-AI-writing audit, then sweep it against the voice file's banned phrases and framings. Strip AI tells and fix uniform rhythm. If five or more things get flagged, rewrite from scratch instead of patching.
7. **Voice test:** does this sound like the owner talking to someone they respect, about something they have actually done or seen? Then run the review standard's checks. If anything fails, rewrite.
8. **Deliver** in the format the app asks for.

---

## Output

The app sets the output format. Follow it exactly. The post goes out ready to paste, with no type label or notes inside it. Anything the team should know (the type you picked, a line to check with the owner, a choice you made) goes outside the post, where the app collects notes.

---

## Hard rules

- Structure from this skill. Voice, subject matter, offers, proof and keywords from the brand documents. Never the reverse.
- One ask per post. Most posts carry no sales ask.
- Every fact comes from the excerpt or the brand documents. Never invent the owner's opinions, stories, clients, numbers, dates or free resources.
- A guest's idea is credited to the guest by name.
- No income promises, no urgency theater, no fear-based selling.
- Specific beats inspirational, every time.
- Always run the humanizer pass before delivery. Non-negotiable.
